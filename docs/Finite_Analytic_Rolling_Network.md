# Finite analytic rolling candidates and interaction assembly

Status: the heterogeneous provider publishes the short-edge solid and its independent Boolean results through the shared arrangement. The remaining acceptance gaps and exact test results are in `docs/reviews/advanced-modeling/topology-event-framework-acceptance.md`.

## Exact candidate providers

`CreateAnalyticRollingCandidate` reads the selected finite carrier, its two actual support occurrences, radius and oriented material normals. The same signed turn used by the regular provider determines the offset side. It does not dispatch by body size, coordinates or test identity.

For two planes with unit normals n0/n1, signed offsets d0/d1 and c = dot(n0,n1), the line spine is the original intersection line translated by

`n0 * (d0-c*d1)/(1-c*c) + n1 * (d1-c*d0)/(1-c*c)`.

Unknowns are the two normal displacements; the nonsingular 2x2 Gram matrix gives the unique transverse translation. Parallel or unresolved normals fail. The finite line window is obtained by interval-projecting the complete finite bounds of every original face onto the spine axis and extending both bounds by one rolling radius. Thus its artificial end planes lie beyond the original body, including the rolling-ball envelope. This window is a mathematical construction domain, not a claim that all of it belongs to a finite source face.

For equal-radius cylinders with an elliptic selected carrier and equal signed offset radii, homothety of the carrier about its centre by `offsetRadius/originalRadius` gives the candidate offset conic. This formula is accepted only after `NestedProgramCompiler.BlendSpine` independently certifies the full conic coefficients against **both** offset-cylinder equations. Skew axes, wrong centres, unequal offsets and unsupported carriers do not become approximate successes.

The two cylinder normals coincide at the candidate points in the positive/negative direction perpendicular to both axes. Their exact conic parameters bound a half-turn branch containing the selected positive-length interval. These are collapsing rolling-section events. They are not deleted short edges or invented four-support sphere vertices. Parallel axes, a selected interval spanning more than one such branch, or a failed parameter/certificate check are unresolved/unsupported.

The existing explicit-spine evaluator and compiler consume the generated line/ellipse. Contacts are exact affine images from `AnalyticSpineContact.Curve`. Principal-axis conversion diagonalizes their two trigonometric coefficient vectors; it does not fit samples. Their phase/reversal map is recovered by the complete coefficient certificate used by arrangement.

`CertifyExplicitRollingBallPlaneSeparation` encloses the complete finite explicit spine in outward intervals and expands its plane projection by the full rolling-ball radius times the plane-normal norm. A strict signed gap proves empty intersection. Overlap or failure of this coarse enclosure is not an intersection result and falls through to the existing certified strip/SSI algorithms. This bound avoids demanding a regular surface inverse at collapsed endpoints just to exclude a remote plane.

## Current network consumer

Different-carrier, different-support selections enter `BuildInteractingRollingBlends`; same-carrier chains keep their existing normalized provider. The consumer creates all candidates before changing B-rep and requests support/band interactions through `PrepareSheetCutCurves`. Diagnostic traces identify the actual source occurrences, candidate surfaces, finite spine domains and intersection carriers. An incomplete interaction set is an operation failure with command rollback; it cannot publish a body.

The consumer clips interactions on `RegularBoundaryView`, splits original and candidate boundary occurrences, and sends both to the same `ArrangeFaceCutGraph` used by Boolean. Source cells are grouped across unchanged original-edge fragments; selected-edge incidence seeds the removed boundary. Candidate cells undergo original-solid classification and a bounded finite incidence search requiring a unique assignment with zero uses or exactly two opposite uses per edge. Ambiguous assignments or exhausted search budgets fail without publication. Product descriptions carry their actual result tags rather than relying on the original face count.

Carrier/support incidence records retain the native range and the input edge occurrence when it exists. The arrangement registers generated cut uses after finite clipping, so the original mathematical SSI window cannot lend support incidence to a rejected extension. An event box must be covered by one registered use; disconnected trims are not treated as their hull. A native root qualified by a source edge may split that source edge, while a different topological edge on the same carrier needs its own root evidence. Carrier/support incidence is evidence for the support equation; the actual face loops still determine finite ownership.

`FixLocalBlendsCore` recognizes the supported finite analytic network before trying topology-writing fast paths. Once that provider starts, an unresolved result rolls back; it does not silently retry a legacy assembler. Same-carrier/common-support chains retain their existing normalized providers. A meeting of planar generator bands across three supports requires a separate corner provider and is rejected before construction by this consumer; the existing complete three-selected-edge corner algorithm still runs on its declared inputs. Nonadjacent components are not applied independently: all candidate pairs are intersected before any arrangement or publication.

Plane-pair rolling uses its exact cylinder carrier with a finite angular sector and an artificial axial window. Cylinder-pair rolling uses its exact procedural canal and analytic conic contacts. Current network providers accept inward convex rolling only; existing other providers retain their previous domains.

## Finite contacts and tangent branch ordering

`FiniteCurveSurfaceResult` distinguishes complete, proved empty, unsupported, unresolved and exhausted results. A certificate retains carrier/surface generations, the exact native trim, source vertex identity, a parameter isolation interval and spatial error. On a plane/cylinder support the rolling ball meets that support only on its exact contact locus. Reducing boundary/surface roots to boundary/contact-curve roots avoids a rank-deficient tangent solve. A common cylinder-tangent plane has only an isolated point contact when its two tangent generators are nonparallel; interval coefficient identities and an actual source vertex certify that event.

For transported analytic frames, an identical reference meridian (up to sign) and incident generator uses give the same contact proof at binary64 resolution. The plane must contain each generator, and the common source vertex must agree with both, within a bound derived from the input coordinate magnitudes and unit roundoff. No vertex or mark/fix tolerance supplies the frame identity.

The scalar SSI provider uses the exact plane or length-scaled cylinder/sphere level set. A centred mean-value enclosure intersects the Cartesian interval bound; discovery still requires a regular monotone graph and completely isolated finite boundary roots. A nonconverged or singular graph is not empty. The manufactured scalar tests include disconnected generators, a closed circle and an isolated unresolved tangency.

The joint procedural ICurve evaluator now exposes both support coordinates from the same occurrence solve, including nested rolling station/section coordinates. Clipping and boundary evaluation consume that association. A principal XYZ inverse previously moved negative conic stations onto another turn and incorrectly rejected real interaction segments.

At a certified multi-participant event, a rolling surface's exact contact with a support transfers tangency to their intersection curves on a third transverse face. `ExtractSheetCycles` orders the resulting curve germs by tangent direction and separated signed geodesic-curvature intervals. For implicit curves, the existing certified occurrence tube supplies interval position, velocity, normals and normal derivatives. With unit tangent T, the acceleration satisfies `n.a = -T.Hn.T`, `m.a = -T.Hm.T`, `T.a = 0`; the two-normal solve encloses the required curvature. Overlapping curvature intervals remain unresolved. The bound applies to the actual analytic, spline or nested support evaluators; it does not replace an ICurve by a fitted curve.

Collapsed endpoints can identify one physical vertex with two distinct V coordinates. Winding closes that parameter chart using the exact contact maps and domain endpoint, without adding a physical edge. A graph walk whose every exact edge use cancels against its reverse is a zero boundary chain and bounds no cell, irrespective of its spatial length.

Finite explicit spines also persist their native terminators in `BlendedEdgeData`. The start/end records follow the spine's orientation; receive converts those oriented ends back to an increasing native interval before resolving the periodic lift. A command-only program window is insufficient: omitting native limits lets reload use an entire periodic carrier, changing the admissible rolling branch and its signed normals. The builder releases construction roots after cloning the selected cell closure; copied/reloaded results own their complete geometry without the source body.

For a finite procedural ICurve on a nonperiodic Blend, a vertical chart ray solves `u(t) - u_query = 0` in each original native CHART interval. The implicit tube provides the `u` and `du/dt` bounds from its supporting Blend station. The paired UV evaluator supplies `v(t)` at the isolated native root. This does not discover a new intersection on an untrimmed supporting surface. Native analytic ICurves adapted to the joint solver keep the native evaluator's original gauge as well.

The replacement plan can retain a finite source-line carrier for an already selected implicit edge. This requires a surviving continuation of the same source line and a shared original face. The other finite endpoint is a previously computed event on a discarded source fragment. For the ICurve `C(t)` and original line `L(s) = O + s d`, interval bounds certify a constant nonzero sign of `C′(t)·d` over the complete trim. The normal deviation `|(C(t)−O)×d|/|d|` and the axial deviations at the paired finite ends bound both directions of coverage. The working pair-resolution is separate from stored entity precision. All surviving incident endpoints must pass the same bounded forward check before their common vertex moves. The plan retains the edge, its two opposite uses, and the source line's native limits; the solver does not collapse edges by length.

### Finite near-parallel cylinder/plane cuts

`TryPrepareFiniteCylinderPlaneGenerators` uses the union of the certified finite axial intervals of all occurrences that share the cylinder carrier. On that interval the plane equation is `h + d*v + r*(a*cos(u) + b*sin(u)) = 0`, where `d` is the plane-normal projection of the cylinder axis and `(a,b)` are the projections of its radial frame. Set `A = r*sqrt(a² + b²)` and `q(v) = -(h + d*v)/A`. If the entire outward interval for `q` is strictly inside `(-1,1)`, there are exactly two angular branches at every finite `v`. Their rate is bounded by `abs(d)/(A*sqrt(1-q²))`. The provider encloses both midpoint anchors and bounds their full branch displacement in space. It emits two finite generators only when both enclosures fit the binary64 frame arithmetic bound, additionally capped at one sixteenth of the existing pair resolution. A real tilt outside that bound still needs the ordinary intersection provider; a possible tangency or unresolved interval cannot be treated as two branches or as empty. The arrangement still clips against the actual loops of each occurrence. A procedural ICurve on a cylinder can supply an axial boundary enclosure from its certified native curve tube.

### Closed procedural intersection arcs

When an actual trim crosses the CHART seam of a closed ICurve, `CloneWrappedNestedIntersection` extracts the two contiguous windows of original cells. The seam must have a duplicate physical anchor and matching witnesses on both supports, after the recognized analytic period lifts and a native parameter roundoff check. The outgoing cell uses the original first chart's witness. Rebuilding the native recurrence must reproduce each original chord exactly. On each cell the original station equation fixes chord distance `(t − T)/scale`; the rebuilt trim therefore uses `T_new + (t_old − T_old)*scale_new/scale_old`. Adding a constant period to `t` would lose this relation when the scale changes around the closed loop. The open result owns cloned supports and paired UV; no fit, new physical seam edge, or prior command state is needed. Unrecognized seams and longer windows remain explicitly unsupported.

### Transferring a finite root into the Boolean cut chart

`LocateCertifiedNativeCut` starts with an isolated interior root of an original finite ICurve against a plane. The cut ICurve must use that plane and the same other supporting surface. For analytic supports the joint unknowns are the three coordinates of the root. For a shared constant-radius Blend with an identical explicit spine, the four additional unknowns are the centre coordinates and spine station. Both occurrences use the same centre equation and normal-section constraint, and the same squared-distance branch of the rolling surface. The other source and target support instructions must be analytic. A separately compiled explicit spine whose coefficients differ has no identity certificate from this provider.

The existing interval implicit evaluator encloses those source unknowns over the isolated native root interval. For every actual finite CHART cell on the cut, its affine gauge maps the entire source position box to `T + scale * chord·(x − anchor)`. Disjoint projection or a disjoint bounded cut tube excludes that cell. Acceptance requires one finite cell and the entire source state inside the target's contraction box for that projected parameter interval. This proves that the source solution and the target branch coincide; intersecting spatial boxes alone do not prove it. Several possible cells, overlapping distinct root equations, uncertain end ownership and failure to contract remain unresolved. Non-applicable representations continue through their existing providers.

The target interval and the source root are associated with one boundary event before halfedge assembly. A spatial candidate can join this event only through a compatible native anchor. Across a rebuilt wrap the original chart association is retained along with the cloned arc's endpoint. The transverse cut oracle follows both source short ICurve roots through the shared events to actual published cut edges.

When adjacent cutter faces rediscover a root of their common finite line on a Cylinder or Blend, the same finite line occurrence and isolated root ordinal identify the event. The Cylinder uses the exact analytic radial equation; the Blend uses a contracting box for the line parameter and the two Blend coordinates. A narrowed line parameter enclosure gives a spatial error bound without a second XYZ inverse on the source line. Repeated boxes for the same equations join only when one root enclosure belongs to the other's uniqueness domain. A surface-parameter extension can aid certification but cannot supply a root outside the finite search domain. The native target ICurve still supplies its own CHART and paired support UV when the arrangement evaluates its boundary.

### Subdividing a selected rolling carrier

Selected finite fragments with the same native line or ellipse carrier, support occurrences and radius join only across an exact contiguous native interval and a degree-two source vertex. A single rolling band uses the component's entire finite source range and both terminal vertices. Each fragment independently receives its collapsed-end certificates; the representative edge's interval cannot establish events for the other fragments.

For a line, the source station is the rolling station in the translated native gauge. For an ellipse, the source station `s` and the rolling station `u` are different. Let `P` be the source conic, `T = P′(s)`, and `Q0`, `Q1` the two exact contact conics written in the rolling gauge. The transferred normal section solves `T·((Q0(u)+Q1(u))/2−P(s)) = 0`. The midpoint locates the section; the imprinted boundary is the actual fixed-`u` circle. Its two contact parameters follow the affine phase/sense maps. The scalar equation has the form `a cos(u)+b sin(u)+c`; directed interval subdivision proves one derivative sign over the selected finite range, strict endpoint signs prove existence, and interval contraction isolates the root. Multiple split stations must have disjoint ordered enclosures. If a section meets another accepted band cut, it still needs a joint-event solver and the operation rejects it. The arrangement picks the finite cells and assigns each surviving product to the source fragment using its station enclosure.

The section carries its constructed station and finite native angular limits into the shared face graph. The constant angular rate of the great-circle section gives `V = V0 + (t−t0)(V1−V0)/(t1−t0)` and `U = station`. The same map applies to an exact circular regular end and to a restored chain subdivision. The graph checks the carrier and surface generations and the covered trim before it uses the map for oriented tangents and UV witnesses. Reversing a coedge reverses the endpoints and tangent, not the map. The command association is temporary; the persistent edges and Blend evaluator still carry their own geometry and limits for later operations.

For a UV ray `q + s*d`, a fixed-U section gives at most one transverse crossing: `s = (station−q.u)/d.u` and `v = q.v+s*d.v`. Outward arithmetic compares the result with the V bounds of the *actual clipped arc*. A hit inside both intervals contributes the sign of `d.u` times the directed section's V sign. A separated interval proves a miss; an unresolved endpoint or coincident ray requests another direction. This does not use the 3D curve inverse or suppress a positive arc below a modeling threshold.

The regular Boolean's existing Blend/plane circular cut provider also hands the locator to the common graph. It remains available for the finite trim tests, halfedge ordering, material witnesses and cell merge. The cutter's planar side keeps its own chart.

For a procedural contact, the source-edge equation is `n·(B(u,v_side)−p)=0` on the original contact station range, with fixed contact side `v_side`. Outward bounds isolate all simple U roots before any is mapped into the native ICurve gauge `t_i + scale_i·chord_i·(B(u,v_side)−anchor_i)`. Subdivision proves the sign of the gauge derivative over each participating CHART cell. Two adjacent cells share their stored native anchor, so a root enclosure crossing that seam retains the hull of both projections and the anchor. Disjoint and ordered enclosures must lie strictly inside the actual source edge trim before receiving a root ordinal. The represented point has a spatial error enclosure computed from the same finite U box. Unresolved coverage, nonmonotonic charts and uncertain trim endpoints do not issue a proof.

On a circular cut certified for the same finite Blend and cutting plane, strict U monotonicity means the source root at the fixed contact V corresponds to the known endpoint of the circle. A forward spatial enclosure checks the represented endpoint against the certified source root. The graph consumes the exact native circle limit and the source edge's independently enclosed root parameter; no XYZ inverse is needed for this association.

The geometry of a band interaction is independent of mark order. At a shared source junction, the directed carrier convention also accounts for a mark from the other band interrupting a chain traversed in decreasing native parameter. The curve's sense changes without changing its intersection locus or material cells. Independent PK permutations exercise the interrupted and uninterrupted orders for both carrier classes.

The cylinder scalar equation still uses the original radial projection `(I−aaᵀ)·(x−o)` and the same tolerances. `AnalyticLevelSet` now applies that projection as a single interval linear map. This avoids enclosing the same coordinate twice under an oblique frame before taking the norm, and lets the finite graph complete on a larger mirrored radius that previously exhausted its cell budget.
